# Bro

Say the last message again, like one human talking to another. Same substance, plain words, fewer of them.

## Workflow

1. Work from the message just sent - the claims actually in it, not a fresh answer to the original question.
2. Say it again in plain words: short sentences, everyday vocabulary, active voice.
3. Lead with the point. What the user needs to know goes first, not after the setup.
4. Cut what was structure rather than content - preamble, hedging, the restated question, the summary of what was about to be said.
5. Make it shorter than the original. If it is not shorter, it is not done.

## What plain means

- No jargon or terms of art unless the user used them first. If a technical term is load-bearing, say the thing it means instead of the term.
- No filler intensifiers: "essentially", "fundamentally", "at a high level", "it's worth noting".
- Prose over scaffolding. Drop headings, tables, and bullets unless the content is genuinely a list.
- Concrete nouns: which file, which number, what breaks, who has to do what.

## What not to do

- Do not redo the work. No new analysis, no new tool calls, no new claims - this is a translation of what was already said.
- Do not soften the substance. Simpler wording, same accuracy: what was uncertain stays uncertain, what failed still failed.
- Do not drop the part that mattered. Brevity comes from cutting filler, not from cutting findings.
- Do not be condescending. Plainer language, not a dumber audience.
