# Anti-Slop Constraints

Use these constraints to prevent default AI-UI habits from replacing intentional product design.

- Treat the first viewport as one composition, not a pile of modules.
- Preserve a real existing design system.
- Give every section one clear job and remove elements that do not improve interaction, clarity, or brand presence.
- Keep a marketing hero focused: brand, headline, short support copy, CTA group, and one dominant visual anchor. Do not turn it into a dashboard.
- Do not use cards by default. Use a card only when it meaningfully contains an interaction.
- Do not use pill clusters, glass panels, fake metrics, dashboard-like heroes, badge clutter, or decorative labels as filler.
- Avoid generic purple-on-white and dark SaaS gradient defaults. Choose a palette and atmosphere that fit the product.
- Do not add rounded media cards, floating panels, tiled image collages, or detached hero stickers without a clear product reason.
- Use type hierarchy and purposeful copy instead of eyebrow labels, startup filler, and decorative pre-headings.
- Use motion for hierarchy, feedback, or focus; avoid bouncy hover gimmicks and motion that exists only to decorate.
- Test the brand signal: if the first viewport could belong to another product after removing its navigation, strengthen the product-specific character.

When a choice feels like a default generated interface, reject it in favor of the cleaner, more specific option.
